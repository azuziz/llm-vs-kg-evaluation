1. Evaluation setups for now:
A. Strict evaluation
- Predicate must match exactly.
- Direction must match (subject → object).
- Hardest evaluation condition.
Purpose:
- Measures exact semantic agreement with the KG.
- Best proxy for factual correctness.

B. Inverse-allowed evaluation
- Predicate inverses counted as correct.
- Direction allowed to flip if predicate inverse exists.
Purpose:
- Corrects for directional ambiguity in biomedical relations.
- Removes penalization for trivial edge reversal.

C. Pair-tolerant, directionless evaluation:
- Predicates collapsed into inverse pairs.
- Direction completely ignored.
- Metrics averaged:
	- across ~25 runs per question,
	- then macro-averaged across 163 questions.
Purpose:
- Measures whether GPT retrieves any correct relation conceptually, ignoring syntactic representation.
- More about semantic discovery than graph exactness.

5. What this means scientifically
Reliability of LLM biomedical relation discovery:
Main takeaway:

LLMs show limited alignment with structured biomedical knowledge graphs, even under relaxed matching conditions.

Specifically:
- Exact relation prediction is rare.
- Direction errors are secondary.
- Predicate selection is the primary challenge.
- Prompt design strongly affects edge production.

Interpretation for thesis (very usable):
- LLMs may capture semantic associations,
- but structured biomedical relation extraction remains unreliable,
- especially compared to curated knowledge graphs.
